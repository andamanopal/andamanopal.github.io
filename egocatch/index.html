A simulated humanoid catches thrown objects it has never seen and holds them in its fingers, with a head-mounted depth camera as its only view of the object.

Everything is trained in NVIDIA Isaac Lab, on a Unitree G1 with two Inspire hands and 512 training objects. First, reinforcement learning trains a privileged controller, which is told exactly how far each part of its hands is from the object's surface and how quickly that gap is closing. That signal is then distilled into a vision network that works it out from the robot's depth camera alone, and the network is fine-tuned on catching itself while the controller stays frozen. On 512 objects it never saw in training, the robot catches about as well this way as when the simulator gives it the exact answer.
